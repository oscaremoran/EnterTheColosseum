Any run of forms — a boss's seven, a Library level, one you built — pays what its forms are worth fought singly, doubled. A single battle pays its own worth. Sine Missione pays triple; a ladder bout pays by the rung. The Ludus pays nothing — wooden swords earn no denarii.

Sponsio — the wager. Three bouts are posted on the board, and you are in none of them. You hold no sword here: you back a man, and then you stand in the stands like everyone else and watch whether you were right.

Arrow keys to move. Stand inside the oculus imperatoris — the emperor's eye, the patch of sand he is watching — to drain your opponent. Only what happens under his eye counts, and the eye keeps moving.

There are two such places. The wide eye drains normally and relocates. The small sub umbone ×III circle sits under your opponent's shield-boss and orbits with them, draining three times as fast — safe ground and fast damage are never in the same place. The crowd knows the difference.

Every red thing costs one hit: Ludus none, Munus Legitimum three, Sine Missione one. The markers at the bottom are what's left, and clearing a bout gives one back. Big attacks flash yellow before they land — the shape you see is the shape that arrives.

Go to the eye. If an eye opens and you never set foot in it before it moves on, your opponent gets an eighth of their health back — the ring turns yellow and counts down first, so you'll see it coming. Waiting in a safe corner for the damage to come to you is not a plan.
