THE EDITOR

He puts on the games. He can be reached.

1 A Word In His Ear ✦ — Your man's opponent is slowed — his throws and everything already in the air crawl for three seconds. Under a tenth of your stake. —
2 The Gates ✦ — Beasts, on him, for the rest of the bout. They do not go back in. About a fifth of your stake. —

Both land on the man you bet against, and both are priced off your stake — so the sum is the same question whether you laid ten or two hundred and fifty. You are no longer betting on what happens; you are paying for it, and the desk prices that in.

This is the career. Twenty-four fighters hold the board, the rudis waits above rank I, and taking it is what this building is asking of you — everything else in the place is training, purse, or spectacle on the way up. The man standing directly above you is put at the top of the day's card as its main event, and he is the only one you may fight for a rung — beat him and you take it. One defeat costs you nothing; lose to him three times running and you drop a rung and he is struck off the card for the day. The rung cannot be dodged: it is fought, or it is still there tomorrow.

Men above you sometimes ask for a bout of their own. Those settle no rung either way and pay like the rung they are standing on, which makes an invitation from near the top the best-paid afternoon available to somebody who has no business being up there yet. You can decline one on the card, and it costs nothing to say no.

Take rank I and the munerarius — the magistrate paying for the games — grants you the rudis, the wooden sword that means discharge.

The board belongs to Munus Legitimum and to nothing else. Under the Ludus there is no rung to win — wooden swords settle nothing — and under Sine Missione there is no climb to have, because one loss would be the end of the career holding it. Take another contract and the board is still here to read; it just has nothing on the day's card and nobody on it you can call out.

Munera · days of games — ▸

A munus was a whole day of games rather than a single bout. These are the parts of one you cannot take in turn. Two to four men come out together and all of them start throwing at the bell.

The emperor only ever watches one of them. That man wears the blue ring and the eye is his, so he is the only one you can do anything about. Everyone else just keeps firing. When he falls the eye moves to whoever is next in line, and the line is not yours to arrange.

Nobody throws at full speed in a crowd. Two men together put out about half again what one man does, and four put out roughly double, not four times. Each of them is slowed to make that come out right, and the more of them there are the slower each one gets.

The granary under the stands, where the codes are kept. Win fights, earn denarii, buy what's on the shelf — then take the code to the Vault.

✦0 denarii

Any run of forms — a boss's seven, a Library level, one you built — pays what its forms are worth fought singly, doubled. A single battle pays its own worth. Sine Missione pays triple; a ladder bout pays by the rung. The Ludus pays nothing — wooden swords earn no denarii.

Sponsio — the wager. Three bouts are posted on the board, and you are in none of them. You hold no sword here: you back a man, and then you stand in the stands like everyone else and watch whether you were right.

THE CARD OFF IN —

The bar under each price is the shape he is in, and the desk has already worked it into the odds. Wounds is whatever is still open on him; it closes a little every day. Fed is his ration. Everyone gets barley, but a man the school has money in eats better than one it does not. Load is the weight of his kit. A provocator in a breastplate burns about a third more of himself per fight than a retiarius in a shoulder-guard does, and takes longer to come back, which is why two men with the same three bouts behind them can be in very different states. The italic line says which of the three is hurting him.

Fighting a card, buying a fresh one, or taking a rung on the Ladder all count as a day passing, and everyone recovers a bit overnight. Letting a card expire does not count. You cannot rest a favourite by standing around.

The stake. Lose and it stays in the sand.

A tessera gladiatoria was a bone tag with a man's name cut into it, carried so there was no argument about whose fights were whose. You can keep several. Your purse, your rungs, the forms you have beaten and the school's notes on everyone else all sit under whichever tag you are holding.

The men on the board
The twenty-four names on the Ladder are real. They come off surviving inscriptions and graffiti, and where a record survives — how many times a man fought, how many he won, how many times he was beaten and allowed to walk off — it is his own, not invented. Glauco's stone was paid for by his wife Aurelia. Bato was a Dacian who fought three men in one day and won the first two. They were people before they were opponents.
The Latin
The contracts, the armaturae, the sparsiones and the rest are the real terms as far as the sources have them. Where the game had to invent a word for something Rome never had to name, it says so in its own comments rather than pretending.

Everything here runs off a disk with no server, no account and nothing sent anywhere. Whatever this game knows about you, it knows only on this machine.

Arrow keys to move. Stand inside the Emperor's Eye, the patch of sand he is watching to drain your opponent's life.

The special ×III Eye rides a wide circle around your opponent, draining three times as fast. But it only burns in bursts: it goes cold, and standing on a cold circle does nothing. What lights it is you not being touched — the ring around it fills as you survive, and a wound empties it back to nothing, wherever on the sand you took it. The ×III is for a fighter nobody is laying a hand on.

Once the ring fills the circle lights, and it is yours: it burns its window down and being hit cannot take it off you. So a mistake costs you the wait for the next burn, never the one you are standing in. Watch the ring spark just before it lights, and be on it when it does.

Go to the eye — at a run. Cross into it at speed and the first moments of the drain are half again as fast, fading as you stand. And do not make a chair of it: sit in one eye for three seconds and it moves on early. The paying style is to arrive fast, take the fat of the window, and go.

If an eye opens and you never set foot in it before it moves on, your opponent gets a piece of their health back. The eye turns yellow and counts down first, so you'll see it coming. Waiting in a safe corner for the damage to come to you is not a plan. The ×III Eye never vanishes, it just goes cold with no punishment for not coming.

Every red thing costs one wound. The markers at the bottom are the number of wounds you can spare, and clearing a bout gives one back. Big attacks flash yellow before they land.

The contracts — a bout is booked as one of three, and the name is the rule. Each has a colour, and it is the colour your marks are drawn in at the foot of the sand, so you can tell what you are risking without looking away from the fight:
● Green — Ludus, the training school. Wooden swords: nothing can wound you, nothing is recorded and nothing is paid. Green is the wood.
● Blue — Munus Legitimum, the sanctioned bout. Three marks. Blue is the emperor's own — the colour of the eye he watches you through.
● Red — Sine Missione, without reprieve. One mark, no appeal will be granted, and triple the purse. Red is blood, and this is the contract that means it.
Sparsiones — the emperor's gifts, thrown into the sand (never red):
● Green — Medicus, the surgeon; puts a lost mark back. At full marks he gives you a spare instead, over your usual three, and you can hold up to three of them. A spare you carry out of a bout is paid for by the magistrate — 2 denarii at Tiro, 5 Gregarius, 7 Veteranus, 12 Secundus Palus, 20 Primus Palus — and carrying two or more doubles the whole purse and brings the stands down on the sand.
● Purple — Filum Parcarum, the Fates' thread; the enemy and his attacks move slower.
● Silver — Talaria, Mercury's winged sandals; you move faster.
